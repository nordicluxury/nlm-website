The Narrative Powers:
Europe's 5 Media Powerhouses, 2026

European media produces the world's most-watched international broadcaster, two of its three major news agencies, the most-used audio platform on earth, and the second-largest television export industry in the world. The Vantage Media Power Index scores who leads and why.

The Vantage · Nordic Luxury Media October 2026 12 min read

European media still sets much of the world's agenda — through broadcasting, publishing, film, games and platforms. Photo: Unsplash

European media is a genuinely global force. The continent produces the world's most-watched international broadcaster, two of its three major news agencies, the most-used audio platform on earth, several of the most-played video games ever made, and the industry that trained Hollywood's directors and supplies much of its production infrastructure. This is not a story of decline. It is a story of distributed, deep, durable power.

The Vantage Media Power Index scores European countries on seven criteria against an American benchmark: publishing and press, broadcasting, film and entertainment, digital platforms, international news reach, industry size, and global influence. Each criterion is scored out of 10. The maximum total is 70.

Five countries stand apart from the rest. Here is how they built their power and what it actually means.

#1
🇬🇧 United Kingdom
60 / 70 · Europe's highest score

London — home to the BBC, Reuters, Pinewood Studios, and the second-largest television export industry in the world. Photo: Unsplash

Britain's score of 60 out of 70 reflects something real. This is the only country in Europe that performs at or near world-class level across almost every category of media simultaneously.

Start with the numbers. Britain's entertainment and media market is forecast to reach £97 billion by 2029, the largest in Europe. Its creative industries employ 2.42 million people. Film and high-end television production reached £6.8 billion in 2025, a record. The BBC's global audience grew to 502 million people per week in 2026, an 11% increase on the year before.

That audience figure is worth dwelling on. 502 million people, every week, choosing to read, watch or listen to something made by a single British institution. That is more than the entire population of the European Union. The BBC World Service alone, broadcasting in over 40 languages, accounts for 355 million of those weekly users.

British television is the second-largest exporter of television content in the world, behind only the United States.

That position is not accidental. It is the result of decades of investment in writers, directors, production companies and studios that together constitute the most productive television industry outside Hollywood. The series that define global streaming culture are disproportionately British. Peaky Blinders, Sherlock, Downton Abbey, The Crown, Black Mirror, Luther, Fleabag, Slow Horses, The Night Manager — these are not niche exports. They are the shows that Netflix, Amazon and Apple have built commissioning relationships with British studios to keep producing.

When the major American streaming platforms decided they needed a European creative base, they came to Britain first. Netflix, Amazon and Apple have all made long-term production commitments in the UK because the writing, the acting talent, the crews and the infrastructure are here at a scale and quality that does not exist anywhere else in Europe at the same level.

Pinewood and Leavesden studios, outside London, are where Star Wars, James Bond, Harry Potter and the Marvel Cinematic Universe were made. The UK's production ecosystem trained generations of directors, cinematographers and crew who now work across the global industry. When Hollywood needs a period drama, a spy thriller or a prestige limited series, the default answer is to make it in Britain.

Then there is Reuters, one of the three wire services that supply most of the world's foreign news. The Financial Times, read in every major financial centre on earth. The Economist, which has shaped the framing of global economics and politics for 183 years. The Booker Prize, the most influential literary award in the English-speaking world. In Brand Finance's Global Soft Power Index, which surveys more than 150,000 people across more than 100 countries, Britain ranks fourth in 2026 — Europe's highest-ranked country.

Britain scores 60 because it has genuinely built all of this. The BBC, Reuters, the FT, Pinewood, the second-largest television export industry in the world, and the creative economy that underpins it. No other European country has assembled all of it at the same time.

#2
🇫🇷 France
53 / 70

Paris — home to AFP, one of three global wire services, and Canal+, now a pay-television group across more than 70 countries. Photo: Unsplash

France's media power is architectural. It built infrastructure that other countries' media depends on to function.

AFP, Agence France-Presse, is one of the three wire services that supply most of the world's daily foreign news. It operates in about 151 countries, maintains 201 bureaux, and earns half its commercial revenue from clients outside France. Every major newsroom in the world has an AFP subscription. Most of what you read about countries in Africa, the Middle East and parts of Asia was gathered by AFP journalists before it was published anywhere else.

France Médias Monde, which runs France 24 and RFI, recorded 278 million weekly contacts in 2025, up 9% on the year before. France 24 broadcasts in four languages including Arabic and English. RFI is the dominant international radio station across Francophone Africa.

Canal+ is Europe's most internationally ambitious pay-television group. After its acquisition of South Africa's MultiChoice, it operates in more than 70 countries with 42.3 million subscribers and combined revenue of approximately €8.7 billion in 2025. Its film production arm, StudioCanal, is one of Europe's most active buyers and distributors of cinema.

Banijay is one of the most commercially powerful television production companies in the world. It makes MasterChef, Big Brother, Survivor and Peaky Blinders. Its formats run in dozens of countries simultaneously. Banijay is French-owned.

France's cultural and creative industries generated €102.7 billion in revenue in 2024, equivalent to 2.9% of French GDP. The Cannes Film Festival remains the most commercially important film market on earth. Ubisoft, based in France, is one of the largest video game publishers in the world, responsible for Assassin's Creed and Far Cry. Hachette Livre is one of the three largest book publishers globally.

France scores 53 because it built real infrastructure at scale, across multiple industries, across multiple continents.

#3
🇩🇪 Germany
47 / 70

Berlin — Germany runs Europe's largest media company, its most-distributed book publisher and one of the continent's most significant international news operations. Photo: Unsplash

Germany's media power is concentrated in ownership and scale. It runs Europe's largest media company and the continent's most globally distributed book publishing group.

Bertelsmann earned €19 billion in revenue in 2025. It owns RTL Group, which operates 56 television and radio channels across Europe. It owns Penguin Random House, the world's largest consumer book publisher, publishing in more than 20 languages and distributing across 98 countries. It owns BMG, one of the world's major music rights companies. Bertelsmann is a single German company with genuine global reach across television, books and music simultaneously.

Axel Springer owns Politico, the most-read political publication in Washington and Brussels, and Business Insider, which has tens of millions of monthly readers across Europe and North America. Axel Springer paid approximately $1 billion for Politico in 2021. That is a German company buying the publication that covers the US Congress.

RTL Group, following its acquisition of Sky Deutschland in June 2026, now runs a pay-television operation with about 12.3 million subscribers in Germany and Austria. ARD and ZDF, Germany's public broadcasters, are two of the highest-funded public media organisations in Europe. Deutsche Welle publishes in about 30 languages. Germany's cultural and creative economy generated €204.6 billion in revenue in 2023.

Germany scores 47 rather than higher because German-language content travels less internationally than English, French or Spanish content. Its industry is large and its ownership is global. Its editorial voice remains largely regional.

#4
🇪🇸 Spain
39 / 70

Madrid — Netflix's first European production centre, and the base for a Spanish-language media industry with a natural audience of 635 million people. Photo: Unsplash

Spain's media power is built on the most commercially valuable thing in this industry: a language.

More than 635 million people speak Spanish as their native or proficient language, according to the Instituto Cervantes 2025 yearbook. That is the world's second-largest native-language community after Mandarin. A Spanish television series, a Spanish news article, or a Spanish song begins with an audience of 635 million people who need no subtitle or translation.

Netflix identified this as an asset before most media analysts did. It chose Madrid for its first dedicated European production centre and has pledged more than €1 billion of investment in Spain between 2025 and 2028. The results have been commercial: Money Heist and Elite became internationally successful series that reached audiences in over 190 countries.

The Madrid region's audiovisual sector now accounts for 2.6% of regional GDP and employs more than 100,700 people directly or indirectly. EFE, the Spanish state news agency, is the leading news service for Spanish-language media globally. Grupo Planeta is one of Europe's largest publishing groups, with major operations across Spain and Latin America. PRISA, which owns El País and Cadena SER, reaches Spanish-language audiences across three continents.

Spain's entertainment and media market is forecast to reach €45.1 billion by 2029. Its combination of language scale, production talent, and growing streaming investment makes it the most dynamic media market in Europe below the top three.

#5
🇸🇪 Sweden
35 / 70

Sweden — a country of 10 million people that runs the world's most-used audio platform and made the best-selling video game in history. Photo: Unsplash

Sweden's media power is entirely digital and entirely real.

Spotify had 761 million monthly active users in the first quarter of 2026. It generated €17.2 billion in revenue in 2025, with €2.2 billion in operating income. It is the world's dominant audio streaming platform and the only European digital platform operating at American scale. That is a company built in Stockholm that now curates what most of the world listens to.

Swedish video game companies are equally significant. Mojang made Minecraft, the best-selling video game in history with more than 300 million copies sold. King made Candy Crush, played by hundreds of millions of daily users. EA DICE, based in Stockholm, makes the Battlefield franchise. Paradox Interactive makes grand strategy games with dedicated global communities. The Swedish games industry generated SEK 36.8 billion in revenue in 2024 from 1,101 companies employing 9,130 people. Games account for approximately 3% of Sweden's total service exports.

Bonnier is a major book and media group with significant operations across the Nordic region, Germany and the United States.

Sweden scores 35 rather than higher because its international news reach is minimal — no global wire service, no international broadcaster of significant scale. Sweden built enormous influence through technology rather than editorial. That is a genuine form of power and a modern one. The world listens to Sweden every day. It just rarely hears Swedish.

What consolidation means for European media

Europe's largest media companies are getting bigger through acquisition. MFE took 75.61% of ProSiebenSat.1, giving an Italian group control over Germany's second commercial broadcaster. RTL closed its acquisition of Sky Deutschland in June 2026. Canal+ absorbed MultiChoice. Banijay merged with All3Media into a production group with approximately €7.4 billion in combined revenue.

The European Media Freedom Act has applied since August 2025, protecting editorial independence and the presence of European media on major platforms. The Commission must present its evaluation of the Audiovisual Media Services Directive by December 2026, with prominence for European content and rules for influencers on the table.

European media in 2026 is powerful, consolidating, and building for scale. The five countries at the top of this index are home to some of the most significant media organisations in the world, measured not by where they are headquartered but by who is actually watching, reading, listening, and playing.
