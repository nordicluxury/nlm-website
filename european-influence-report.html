From the report
Headline Finding

The United Kingdom ranks first, ahead of France and Germany. Its GDP is Europe's third-largest. Its lead rests on a combination no other European state currently holds: a permanent UN Security Council seat, an independent nuclear deterrent, and one of the world's two truly global financial centres.

This was the closest call in the ranking. France has the more complete EU-integrated institutional position and a broader military footprint through its overseas territories. A different analyst could reasonably swap the top two.

Biggest Surprise

Belgium ranks 9th, ahead of Sweden, Norway and Denmark. Its economy and military are smaller than all three. It ranks above them because Brussels hosts both the EU's main institutions and NATO's headquarters, and that single form of leverage outweighs a weaker score everywhere else.

Below roughly rank 35, the gaps between neighbouring entries narrow to the point where small methodology choices could reorder several positions. The report states plainly where judgment calls were made.

The eight criteria
01
Economic Weight and Capital Control
GDP, financial centre status and capital moved through the country.
02
Political and Institutional Power
Whether decisions made here bind people elsewhere.
03
Cultural Export
Fashion, art, music, cuisine and ideas voluntarily adopted elsewhere.
04
Media and Information Reach
Whether the country sets the global news agenda or shapes opinion abroad.
05
Talent and Capital Gravity
Whether ambitious, wealthy or skilled people relocate here specifically for the access.
06
Connectivity and Infrastructure
Physical and digital trade and transport routes running through the country.
07
Diplomatic and Network Centrality
Embassies, alliances, treaties and summits based here.
08
Durability of Influence
Whether the influence has held for decades or is a recent and possibly fragile spike.
